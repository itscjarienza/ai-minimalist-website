The Problem

Most teams have no clean answer to the question: did AI make this?

When you publish at speed — newsletters, posts, case studies, client deliverables — the trail gets thin. AI helps draft, edit, rewrite, and generate. But there's rarely a record of how, who checked it, or whether anyone decided whether that matters for this specific asset.

That gets messy fast when:

  • A client asks how their deliverable was produced
  • A platform changes its disclosure requirements
  • Something goes out that should have had a second look

The fix isn't a policy document nobody reads. It's one simple row added to each piece of content before it publishes.

What It Is

A lightweight workflow sheet, not a compliance document.

01
Mark whether AI was used and how.

For each asset type — newsletter, post, case study, image, sales collateral — your team marks yes or no and notes the role AI played. Drafting, editing, summarizing, generating.

// one field. takes five seconds. creates a record that didn't exist before.

02
Decide if disclosure is needed.

Not every AI-assisted asset needs a label. The SOP gives you a simple frame for making that call — based on asset type, audience, and context — so your team isn't guessing each time.

03
Name the approver before anything publishes.

No publish without a named person. One field on the form. This eliminates the most common failure mode: content goes out because nobody stopped it, not because someone approved it.

// "the team" is not an approver. a name is an approver.

04
Log the decision somewhere your team can find it.

Content log, Notion, a shared sheet — it doesn't matter where. What matters is that the decision exists outside of someone's memory. The SOP captures where that archive lives.
