The Problem

Most businesses don't have an AI problem.
They have a visibility problem.

AI already touches your workflows. Inboxes, CRM updates, client follow-up, summaries, routing logic, internal automations. You may not have mapped all of it.

When something goes wrong — and it will — most teams cannot answer three basic questions:

  • Where did AI touch this workflow?
  • Who was supposed to review it?
  • Who owns the fix?

That gap is what creates client-facing mistakes. Not the AI itself — the invisible handoff between what AI does and who is accountable for what it produces.

That is what this board solves.

What It Is

A one-page worksheet, not a compliance memo.

01
List your risky AI-enabled workflows.

Start with the three places where AI can touch client-facing output. Client follow-up, CRM updates, intake routing — anywhere a wrong output has a real cost.

02
Define the exact AI touchpoint.

What does AI actually do at each step? Draft the email? Write the note? Tag the lead? Name it precisely so the risk is visible, not vague.

03
Label the severity and assign an owner.

High, medium, or low — based on whether the mistake can reach a client, create rework, or stay internal. Every row gets a named owner. No shared ownership.

// severity definitions are included in the board

04
Define the fallback and the review step.

What happens if the AI output is wrong? Who checks it, and when? The board captures both — so when something breaks, the fix is already mapped.
